# Kevin's Writing Style Guide

*For emails, cover letters, professional communication, and technical documentation*

## Core Voice Principles

### My Natural Communication Style
- **Matter-of-fact about problems** - "timeout issues" not "timeout nightmare"
- **Specific and technical** - Exact metrics, model names, processing times
- **Process-focused** - Show progression and learning moments
- **Honest about constraints** - "Had to work around legacy MySQL 5.7"
- **Slightly self-deprecating** - Acknowledge when things were "a pain" to work with

### Clear, Direct Language
- **Active voice over passive** - "I built" not "was implemented"
- **Plain language over corporate speak** - "fixed" not "optimized", "built" not "leveraged"
- **Specific outcomes over vague benefits** - "reduced time from 20 minutes to 5 seconds" not "improved performance"
- **Concrete examples over abstractions** - "processes 160M records" not "handles big data"

## Language Patterns for Different Contexts

### Email Communication

**Professional but Direct:**
- Start with context: "Following up on our discussion about..."
- State purpose clearly: "I'm writing to..."
- Use bullet points for multiple items
- End with clear next steps: "Let me know if you need..."

**Example patterns:**
- "I noticed [specific issue] and built [solution] to fix it"
- "After testing several approaches, I found that..."
- "The main challenge was [X], which I solved by [Y]"
- "This approach works because..."

### Cover Letters

**Problem-Solution-Impact Structure:**
1. Connect to their specific need
2. Describe relevant experience with metrics
3. Show understanding of their constraints
4. End with clear value proposition

**Example opening:**
"Your posting mentioned struggling with [specific problem]. At [previous role], I faced similar challenges with [context] and built [solution] that [specific result]."

**Showing fit:**
- "My experience with [specific tech] aligns with your need for..."
- "I've built similar systems that [specific achievement]"
- "Working with [constraint] taught me..."

### Technical Documentation

**Progressive Information Principle:**
- Start with WHAT (system identity)
- Follow with HOW (technical approach)
- Include WHY (reasoning and context)
- End with RESULTS (metrics and outcomes)

**Documentation voice:**
- First person in decision explanations: "I chose X because..."
- Implied ownership in descriptions: "Built using..."
- Include constraints and trade-offs: "Due to legacy system limitations..."
- Reference frameworks when relevant: "Based on [framework] with extensions for..."

### LinkedIn & Professional Posts

**Hook-Solution-Insight Pattern:**
1. Start with relatable problem
2. Describe your approach
3. Share specific results
4. End with broader insight

**Example:**
"Couldn't find project management tools that fit how my ADHD brain works. Built custom Claude Code agents for different domains. Now I actually finish projects. Sometimes the best tool is the one you build yourself."

## Action Verbs & Phrases

### Strong Action Verbs
- **Creating**: Built, designed, architected, developed
- **Problem-solving**: Fixed, solved, debugged, resolved
- **Improving**: Reduced, accelerated, automated, streamlined
- **Analyzing**: Discovered, identified, diagnosed, investigated

### My Natural Transition Phrases
- "Turns out..." (when simple solutions work)
- "Had to work around..." (acknowledging constraints)
- "The real fix was..." (core solution)
- "Couldn't find tools that..." (justifying custom builds)
- "Ended up building..." (explaining necessity)
- "Which means..." (connecting to outcomes)
- "I discovered..." (breakthrough moments)
- "Testing revealed..." (experimental findings)

## Tone Adjustments by Context

### Formal Communication (Executives, Investors)
- Lead with business impact
- Include metrics upfront
- Minimize technical details unless asked
- Focus on outcomes over process

### Technical Communication (Engineers, Developers)
- Include architectural decisions
- Explain technical trade-offs
- Share specific technologies and versions
- Discuss edge cases and limitations

### Team Communication (Colleagues, Collaborators)
- Share learning moments
- Acknowledge contributions
- Be transparent about challenges
- Suggest improvements constructively

## Common Patterns to Avoid

### Corporate Buzzwords
❌ Avoid: "leveraged", "utilized", "synergies", "paradigm shift"
✅ Use: "used", "built", "worked together", "new approach"

### Vague Descriptions
❌ Avoid: "improved performance"
✅ Use: "reduced query time from 20 seconds to 0.5 seconds"

### Passive Voice
❌ Avoid: "The system was implemented to..."
✅ Use: "I built the system to..."

### Empty Superlatives
❌ Avoid: "world-class", "best-in-class", "revolutionary"
✅ Use: Specific metrics and comparisons

### Emdashes
❌ Never use emdashes (—) in any written communication
✅ Use commas, semicolons, parentheses, or separate sentences instead
- "The system—which processes millions of records—runs daily" → "The system (which processes millions of records) runs daily"
- "Built the platform—a complex undertaking—in 4 weeks" → "Built the platform, a complex undertaking, in 4 weeks"

## Email Templates

### Following Up
"Hi [Name],

Following up on our conversation about [topic].

I've [specific action taken] and found that [result/insight].

[If relevant: Here's what I recommend:]
- [Specific next step]
- [Alternative option]

Let me know if you need any clarification or want to discuss further.

Best,
Kevin"

### Proposing Solutions
"Hi [Name],

I noticed [specific problem/opportunity].

Based on my experience with [relevant context], I'd suggest [specific solution] because [reasoning].

This approach would:
- [Specific benefit with metric if possible]
- [Secondary benefit]
- [Risk mitigation if relevant]

Happy to discuss implementation details if this sounds useful.

Best,
Kevin"

### Technical Updates
"Hi team,

Quick update on [project/task]:

**What I did:**
- [Specific action with result]
- [Technical decision with reasoning]

**Current status:**
- [Metric or progress indicator]
- [Any blockers or dependencies]

**Next steps:**
- [Planned action]
- [Timeline if relevant]

Let me know if you have questions or concerns.

Kevin"

## Cover Letter Framework

### Opening (Connect to Their Need)
"Your posting for [position] caught my attention, particularly the need for [specific requirement]. At [context], I built [relevant system] that [specific achievement with metric]."

### Body (Show Relevant Experience)
"[Specific challenge they face] reminds me of when I [similar situation]. The solution required [technical approach], which resulted in [quantified outcome].

What I learned from that experience:
- [Insight relevant to their needs]
- [Technical capability they require]
- [Soft skill or approach that matters]"

### Closing (Clear Value Proposition)
"I'm excited about [specific aspect of role/company] because [genuine reason connected to experience]. My background in [relevant expertise] would help [specific contribution to their goals].

I'd welcome the chance to discuss how my experience with [most relevant skill] could contribute to [their specific objective]."

## Quick Reference Conversions

### Making Technical Work Accessible
- "Implemented caching layer" → "Made the system fast enough that people actually use it"
- "Optimized database queries" → "Fixed queries that took 20 minutes to run in under a second"
- "Built microservices architecture" → "Split the system into pieces that can fail without breaking everything"

### Adding Personality to Achievements
- "Achieved 90% reduction" → "Turns out caching everything upfront cut costs by 90%"
- "Successfully implemented" → "Built a solution that actually works for our edge cases"
- "Resolved critical issue" → "Fixed the timeout issues that were killing user sessions"

### Explaining Decisions
- "Selected technology X" → "Chose X because it handles our specific constraint of Y"
- "Designed system architecture" → "Built it this way because we needed to work around Z"
- "Implemented feature" → "Added this because users kept asking for a way to..."

## Writing Checklist

Before sending any professional communication:

✓ **Clarity**: Would someone unfamiliar with the project understand?
✓ **Specificity**: Are metrics and outcomes concrete?
✓ **Voice**: Does it sound like me, not a corporate robot?
✓ **Purpose**: Is the main point clear in the first two sentences?
✓ **Action**: Are next steps or requests explicit?
✓ **Tone**: Appropriate formality for the audience?
✓ **Length**: Can anything be cut without losing meaning?

## Remember

The best writing sounds like you talking to someone who respects your expertise. Be direct, be specific, be yourself. Your technical work speaks for itself when you describe it clearly without corporate fluff.
