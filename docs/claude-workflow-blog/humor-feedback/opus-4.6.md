### 1. Introduction

**Location:** After "The code is almost an afterthought."

**Suggestion:** "If that sounds backwards, congratulations — you've been writing code like a normal person. I was too, until I realized the expensive part was never the typing."

**Why it works:** Self-deprecating acknowledgment that this workflow sounds weird, disarms skeptics before they check out.

### 2. Introduction - Session Count

**Location:** "This post took [X] sessions using the exact workflow I'm about to describe."

**Suggestion:** Replace [X] with the real number, then add: "Yes, I used an AI workflow to write a post about AI workflows. God creates dinosaurs, God destroys dinosaurs, God creates man, man creates AI, AI writes blog post about itself. Goldblum inherits the earth."

**Why it works:** Gets the meta-humor out front and adapts one of the most quotable JP chains. Audience is already thinking it — say it first.

### 3. Phase 1: Discussion - The Opening Prompt

**Location:** After Hammond shares his documentation and says "Just need a fresh set of eyes before we announce to investors."

**Suggestion:** Add a Kevin aside: *"What do they got in there, King Kong?" No. Worse. A codebase with no tests and a launch date.*

**Why it works:** Bridges the JP spectacle to developer dread — every dev has inherited a codebase like this. The anticlimax from King Kong to "no tests" is the joke.

### 4. Phase 1: Discussion - Nedry Mention

**Location:** After "...and why one IT employee seemed to have credentials for every critical system."

**Suggestion:** Add in italics: *Claude paused here for a long time. In Claude terms, this means roughly 400 milliseconds. In human terms, it means "are you serious."*

**Why it works:** Anthropomorphizes Claude just enough to be funny, and the milliseconds/human-terms contrast is a clean quip. Also foreshadows Nedry being a problem without over-explaining it.

### 5. Phase 2: Plan Review - Autosolve Section

**Location:** After the autosolve list, specifically after "life finds a way"

### 6. Phase 3: Best Idea - Research Results

**Location:** After "They don't just react; they experiment."

**Suggestion:** Add: *If this sounds like your LLM systematically testing the boundaries of its system prompt, that's because intelligence is intelligence. The fences are different. The probing isn't.*

**Why it works:** This is the sweet spot — AI-as-raptor parallel delivered deadpan. Developers who've watched Claude find creative workarounds to constraints will feel this in their bones.

### 7. Phase 4: Improve Idea - Stretch Section

**Location:** After "Had one nibble at me once. Are we sure they're safe?" / "Only two reported cases..."

**Suggestion:** Finish the exchange: *Hammond: Only two? Claude: Both fatal. Hammond: ... Claude: Moving on to implementation.*

**Why it works:** The abrupt pivot from "two fatal cases" to "moving on" is classic deadpan. Mirrors the real workflow experience where you flag a risk, the user ignores it, and you document that you tried.

**Meme potential:** The "they do move in herds" scene but captioned with a terminal showing multiple Claude agents queued up waiting for approval.

### 9. Code Review - Door Handles Finding

**Location:** After "Replace with round knobs or push-bar mechanisms."

**Suggestion:** Add a Kevin aside: *This is the code review finding that looks trivial until you remember the kitchen scene. Every "minor" finding is a velociraptor learning to open doors. Fix it now or chase it through the kitchen later.*

**Why it works:** The kitchen/door-handle scene is iconic, and "chase it through the kitchen later" is exactly what debugging a "minor" issue in production feels like. Connects JP to a universal dev experience.

### 10. Outro

**Location:** After "The hours of upfront discussion feel slow. Turns out, they pay back in code that doesn't need to be rewritten."

**Suggestion:** *Or as Dr. Malcolm would put it: your prompt engineers were so preoccupied with whether they could one-shot it, they didn't stop to think if they should.*

**Why it works:** The most iconic JP line, saved for the thesis statement. Reframes the whole post's argument in six seconds. Lands harder because you held it back until the end.

**Meme potential:** Goldblum lean meme, captioned: "Your prompt engineers were so preoccupied with whether they could one-shot it..."
