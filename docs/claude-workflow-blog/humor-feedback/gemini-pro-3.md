Here are 10 humor insertions tailored for your Jurassic Park/Claude Code crossover post.

### 1. The TL;DR / Introduction

**Location:** The upfront investment is real. But it pays back in code that doesn't need to be rewritten.

**Suggestion:** Because your developers were so preoccupied with whether or not they *could* build it, they didn't stop to think if they *should* document it.

**Why it works:** Sets the tone immediately with an iconic Ian Malcolm paraphrase that strikes at the heart of engineering hubris.

**Meme potential:** Ian Malcolm looking over his sunglasses, text: "Your engineers were so preoccupied with whether they *could*..."

### 2. Phase 1: Discussion (Context Loading)

**Location:** Hammond has shared his documentation. Containment systems, tour design, staffing plans, the investor deck. Claude has read everything.

**Suggestion:** Claude digested the technical architecture faster than Mr. DNA explaining genetics to a room full of bored tourists.

**Why it works:** A nostalgic nod to the movie's exposition dump, drawing a parallel to how fast AI consumes massive context windows.

### 3. Splitting into Agents (Handoff)

**Location:** This creates a folder in `docs/`, splitting up the plan into task files scoped for smaller agents like Sonnet (faster, cheaper, and they don't need the whole picture).

**Suggestion:** They're coding in parallel. *They do code in parallel.*

**Why it works:** A geeky twist on Sam Neill's breathless realization ("They do move in herds"), highlighting the magic of parallel agent execution.

### 4. Phase 2: Plan Review (The Brutal Truth)

**Location:** This reviewer doesn't remember you talking yourself into "it'll probably be fine." It just sees the plan and asks: does this actually make sense?

**Suggestion:** It looks at your "clever" architectural shortcuts and casually observes, *"That is one big pile of sh\*t."*

**Why it works:** Captures the humbling experience of an AI roasting your poorly-thought-out system architecture.

**Meme potential:** Ian Malcolm standing next to a giant pile of triceratops dung labeled "My V1 Architecture."

### 5. Findings Resolution (Power Systems)

**Location:** **Impact:** All electric fencing fails simultaneously across every paddock.

**Suggestion:** And suddenly, the company lawyer is re-evaluating his choice of restrooms.

**Why it works:** A dark-humor callback to the movie's most famous casualty, perfectly illustrating the catastrophic downstream effects of a single point of failure.

### 6. Finding 4: Viewing Areas (Spectacle vs. Safety)

**Location:** We need elevation for safety, but the experience has to stay visceral. Open air, close enough to feel it.

**Suggestion:** What do they got in there, King Kong? No, just a massive liability case and a PR nightmare waiting to happen.

**Why it works:** Malcolm's cynical quip fits perfectly with the friction between "cool product features" and "infosec compliance."

### 7. Phase 3: Best Idea

**Location:** Run the command with your half-formed thought. Claude does extra research and either confirms its recommendation or finds something it completely missed...

**Suggestion:** This is the phase where you find out if your prompt engineered a docile herbivore, or if you have to ask yourself, *"You bred raptors?"*

**Why it works:** Compares the unpredictable output of generative AI to unintended genetic mutations. 

### 8. Phase 4: Improve Idea (Stretch Goals)

**Location:** The wild ideas aren't always practical, but sometimes they're exactly what the project needs.

**Suggestion:** Sometimes it suggests a brilliant UI overhaul. Sometimes it suggests feeding a live goat to your routing middleware just to see what happens.

**Why it works:** A deadpan escalation of how weird AI hallucinations or "creative" suggestions can get when you ask it to stretch.

### 9. Implementation (Parallel Execution)

**Location:** I set it running and context-switch to other work. Come back when it's done. (Assuming you've set up the permissions. Ask me how I learned that.)

**Suggestion:** Let's just say a runaway agent modifying your root directory is the digital equivalent of Timmy getting electrocuted on the perimeter fence.

**Why it works:** Validates the user's parenthetical warning with a visceral, high-stakes metaphor for giving AI too much terminal access.

### 10. Outro (The Symbiosis)

**Location:** Claude sees patterns across millions of codebases. You see the user who's been asking for that feature for six months. Neither perspective is complete. The workflow exists to collide them.

**Suggestion:** Developer writes prompt. Prompt creates AI agent. AI agent writes code. Code breaks production. Developer inherits the earth. 

**Why it works:** A perfect adaptation of the Malcolm/Sattler dialogue ("God creates dinosaurs..."), bringing the central theme of AI anxiety vs. human judgment to a satisfying close.
