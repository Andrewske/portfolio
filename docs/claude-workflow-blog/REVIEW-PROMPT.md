### How to Review

**Read as this person:** A skeptical engineer who's heard "AI writes my code" claims before and rolled their eyes. They're curious but not sold.

**Be direct.** "This section is boring" is more useful than "this section could perhaps be tightened."

**First impression:** After your first skim, note your gut reaction before deep reading. What did you want to skip? What pulled you in?

**The "so what" test:** For each section, ask: does this justify its length? Could it be cut in half without losing value?

**Quotable moments:** Which lines would you screenshot and share? Which made you smirk?

**Missing content:** What questions does a reader have after finishing that weren't addressed?

**Anti-patterns to flag:** Watch for "Claude is amazing!" vibes, tutorial-speak ("simply do X"), or unexplained jargon.

**Dual-track consideration:** Do the Kevin sections work if you skip all JP content? Is there context missing that only exists in the dinosaur narrative?

## Background

This is a blog post for Kevin's portfolio explaining his Claude Code workflow. The post uses a Jurassic Park narrative where John Hammond works with Claude to plan the park's opening.

### Why This Post Exists

Kevin's coworkers at Glade.ai are curious about his AI-assisted development process. Rather than explaining it repeatedly, he's writing a comprehensive guide that shows:
- The specific workflow phases (Discussion → Plan → Review → Best-idea → Improve → Implement → Code-review)
- Why each phase exists and what breaks when you skip it
- How human judgment combines with Claude's capabilities

### Target Audience

- **Primary**: Engineers curious about AI-assisted development
- **Secondary**: Medium readers who might discover it

### Core Thesis

**"Plans are the product."** Heavy investment in planning, review, and iteration before touching code is what makes Claude write code you trust.

Supporting ideas:
- "Mind fusion" - Neither Claude nor human writes the plan alone. Claude has breadth, you have domain knowledge. The collision creates better plans.
- "Ask questions one at a time" - Forces thorough exploration before rushing to solutions
- Human corrections matter - Claude recommends based on what it sees; you fill in what it can't

## Key Decisions (Don't Suggest Changing These)

These were intentional choices after multiple planning sessions:

### Structure
- **Dinos/No-Dinos toggle** - Kevin sections work as standalone tutorial; JP sections are optional entertainment
- **`[KEVIN]` markers** - Indicate narrator commentary sections (will be styled differently in final)

### Narrative
- **Third-person observer** - We watch Hammond + Claude interact
- **Hammond's arc** - Starts overconfident ("spared no expense"), becomes collaborative
- **JP knowledge assumed** - References land without explanation (everyone's seen the movie)
- **Highlight reel approach** - Show key phases in depth, brief interludes for functional phases

### Tone
- Practical and direct in Kevin sections
- Self-aware humor in JP sections
- Shows "why" without being preachy
- Confident but not dogmatic

### Style (Kevin's Writing Preferences)
- No em-dashes (use commas, semicolons, parentheses, or separate sentences)
- Natural transition phrases ("Turns out...", "Instead...")
- Direct language over flowery descriptions
- JP quotes as standalone quips, not setups requiring explanation
